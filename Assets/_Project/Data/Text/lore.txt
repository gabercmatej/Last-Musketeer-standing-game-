Paris, 1627. Long before anyone wrote books about musketeers, there were four of them: <b>Matej, Matic, Aljaž and Nik</b>. <i>Los Cuatro Mosqueteros.</i> Sworn brothers. One for all, all for one. They drank from the same tankard, shared the last croissant (mostly), and saved the King exactly once, by accident.

Then came the night at the tavern <i>Le Coq Ivre</i>. The King's messenger arrived with a single golden plume: the Captain's Feather, to be worn by the greatest musketeer in all of France. Just one. Four hands reached for it at the same time. Words were said. Someone mentioned hair. Someone mentioned abs. Someone threw a powder keg (Nik insists it was a cheese wheel). By dawn the tavern had no roof, and the brotherhood had no future.

Now they meet in the Cardinal's crumbling courtyard and on the rotten bridges over the Seine to settle it the only way musketeers know how: muskets, rapiers, kegs of extremely questionable gunpowder, and absolutely no friendship. The ground falls away beneath their boots. The Crown goes to whoever is winning, and paints a target on his head. And when the smoke clears, only one name will be remembered.

<i>All for one… and one for… actually, just me.</i>
